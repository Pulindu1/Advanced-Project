# Participant Information Sheet

**Study title:** Evaluating a Web Exploitation CTF Suite for
Undergraduate Cybersecurity Education

**Researcher:** Pulindu Fonseka, MEng Computer Science, Durham
University (cfcf66@durham.ac.uk)

**Supervisor:** David Oswald

---

## Invitation

You are invited to take part in a 90-minute user study as part of
the researcher's MEng dissertation. Please read this sheet before
deciding whether to participate.

## Purpose

The study evaluates a suite of Capture-the-Flag (CTF) cybersecurity
challenges designed for undergraduate learners. We want to understand
how real players approach the challenges, where they get stuck, and
what they learn.

## What the session involves

1. A short pre-study survey (5--10 min) about your programming and
   cybersecurity background.
2. Three challenges from the suite, up to 18 minutes each, with a
   facilitator watching and offering timed hints.
3. A post-study survey (~10 min) about workload and perceived
   learning.
4. Screen and microphone recording during the session.

## Voluntariness

Participation is voluntary. You may stop at any time without giving a
reason. You may withdraw your data up to 14 days after the session
by emailing the researcher with your participant code.

## Data handling

- You are assigned a participant code (P01..P10). Your name appears
  only on the consent form, stored separately from session data.
- Recordings are kept on encrypted university storage and destroyed
  once the dissertation is marked.
- The dissertation may report aggregate statistics and short
  redacted excerpts that do not identify you.

## Risks and benefits

No physical risk. Some challenges are technically demanding and may
be frustrating; you can take breaks or stop at any point. No direct
benefit to you beyond contributing to educational research.

## Approvals

Durham University Department of Computer Science low-risk ethics
committee.

## Questions

Email the researcher or supervisor named above at any time.
