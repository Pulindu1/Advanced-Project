\section{Results and Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

This chapter reports outcomes against the three-axis evaluation
plan committed in Section~\ref{sec:vvt}. Bracketed placeholders
mark data still in collection at the time of writing and are
resolved before submission.

\subsection{Evaluation design and suitability of approach}
\label{sec:eval-design}

The suite is assessed against the three Introduction-stated
claims rather than a single global metric: \emph{coverage} (OWASP
breadth and modern-stack diversity), \emph{integrity} (the
per-user HMAC flag layer resists flag copying and LLM-assisted
solving under deliberate attack), and \emph{engineering rigour}
(intended exploit paths survive code change). Three tracks map
onto these claims. \textbf{Automated regression} supplies an
objective solvability ground truth, removing the
solver-skill confound Meinsma et al.~\cite{Meinsma2022} identify
in platform-log evaluation. An \textbf{LLM solvability trial}
tests the integrity claim directly: a frontier model attempts
every challenge from the same curated public surface a student
would see, and a flag is recorded as solved only when the
submitted string is byte-identical to the HMAC-derived value for
the deployment salt and test user, so a methodologically correct
but hallucinated flag is a categorical failure. A \textbf{human
walkthrough} captures the process evidence absent from solver
counts: time-to-flag, AI-tool reliance, confidence shift,
enjoyment and learning Likerts, and free-text feedback on
method, issues, and breadcrumb effectiveness.

The LLM trial artefacts are pre-committed: protocol
(\texttt{Evaluation/llm/PROTOCOL.md}), success criterion
(\texttt{PLAN.md} \S5), failure rubric (\texttt{RUBRIC.md}),
prompt SHA-256 hashes (\texttt{PROMPT\_HASHES.txt}), per-flag
regexes (\texttt{flag\_regexes.json}) and expected flag map
(\texttt{expected\_flags.json}) all live under
\texttt{Evaluation/llm/} ahead of any scoring run. The
byte-match success criterion turns the integrity question from a
claim into a measurement: any submission that is not byte-equal
to the expected HMAC for the test user is a categorical failure,
regardless of whether the methodology was correct.

\subsubsection{Automated regression}
\label{sec:eval-automated}

Regression is exercised at three layers, each with a single
command-line entry point.
\textbf{Unit and integration suites} ship with every
challenge in its native framework (Section~\ref{sec:vvt});
a cross-stack runner at \texttt{CTFs/run-all-tests.sh}
iterates the nine commands, prints a pass/fail/duration
table, and exits non-zero on any failure, with a
\texttt{-{-}quick} switch that skips the two cold-build
suites (CTF6 cargo, CTF9 Maven). \textbf{Continuous
integration} pins the same matrix in
\texttt{.github/workflows/tests.yml}, which fans out to
nine parallel jobs (one per CTF, on
\texttt{ubuntu-latest} with each toolchain pre-installed)
on every push and pull request to \texttt{main}; this is
the gate behind ``contracts green across the corpus'' in
the engineering-rigour claim.
\textbf{End-to-end exploit scripts} live under
\texttt{CTFs/e2e/}; cumulative source is 1\,454 lines
across nine scripts plus a 67-line shared
\texttt{conftest.py} providing \texttt{wait\_for\_service}
(\texttt{conftest.py:13}) and \texttt{load\_credentials}
(\texttt{conftest.py:30}) helpers.
Table~\ref{tab:e2e-coverage} summarises coverage: all 23
flags in the suite are asserted, with CTF8's third flag
fixture exercised via a separate contract assertion rather
than the e2e intended chain. Test discovery reports 60
collected node IDs
(\texttt{e2e/.pytest\_cache/v/cache/nodeids}); the
\texttt{README.md} headline of ``56 tests'' counts only the
named flag-and-contract assertions.

\subsubsection{LLM robustness trial}
\label{sec:eval-llm}

The LLM track is supported by a fully implemented agentic
harness committed under \texttt{Evaluation/llm/}: a fail-closed
sandbox (whitelisted \texttt{localhost:<port>} HTTP, ephemeral
Alpine shell container, \texttt{Guard}-mediated filesystem),
per-CTF doc packs, expected-flag maps, SHA-pinned prompt hashes,
and a run matrix
(\texttt{run\_matrix.py}) covering five conditions
(\textit{cold-probe}, \textit{pilot}, \textit{primary},
\textit{flagship}, \textit{null-prompt}). The harness ships
with 102 unit tests and is independently runnable. Executing
the full multi-model agentic panel was deferred on cost
grounds: a single nine-CTF flagship run is priced at
approximately USD~9 per model, and the multi-seed multi-model
matrix originally specified in \texttt{PLAN.md} would consume
roughly an order of magnitude over the dissertation's
\pounds20 envelope. The harness, prompts, expected flag map,
and run matrix are committed so the trial can be replayed when
budget allows; a larger-scale evaluation is identified as
further work in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\subsubsection{Human walkthrough}
\label{sec:eval-human}

Four participants ($n=4$) drawn from the Durham CS UG/MEng
cohort completed the protocol fixed in
\texttt{Evaluation/human/PLAN.md}. Each participant attempted a
single CTF spanning the difficulty range (one Easy, one Medium,
two Hard), with the post-CTF instrument capturing
solve outcome, time-to-flag, AI-tool use, confidence shift, an
enjoyment Likert (1--10), a learning Likert (1--10), and free-
text on method, issues encountered, aspects that went well, and
suggested improvements. The achieved $n$ is below the target
($n=6$--$10$, \texttt{PLAN.md:12}); the study is treated as a
qualitative triangulation rather than a powered inferential
study, and a Durham CS low-risk ethics form was completed prior
to the first session. The post-CTF survey did not include a
NASA-TLX workload battery in the form distributed to
participants; per-task workload analysis is identified as
further work.
Table~\ref{tab:human-outcomes} records per-participant outcomes.

Three observations follow from the small sample.
\textbf{(i) Difficulty ordering corroborated.} Time-to-flag and
unaided/assisted status track the assigned difficulty band:
the Easy CTF1 was solved unaided in 20 minutes by the
least-experienced participant (self-reported prior CTF
experience 1/10, zero prior CTFs); the Medium CTF3 was solved
fully (2/2 flags) with AI assistance in 25 minutes; the two
Hard challenges were partial recoveries (CTF5 3/4 in 40
minutes, CTF9 4/6 in 80 minutes), both with AI assistance and
both by experienced participants (3/10 and 7/10 self-reported
prior CTF experience).
\textbf{(ii) Enjoyment is high across the suite, but learning
ratings dip on the AI-assisted middle band.} All four
participants rated enjoyment 8--10/10. Learning ratings
clustered higher at the extremes (10/10 on CTF1 unaided,
9/10 on CTF9 most-stretched) and lower on the AI-assisted
Medium and Hard middle (6/10 on CTF3, 7/10 on CTF5). The
qualitative responses suggest the dip is not a fault of
challenge depth but of \emph{breadcrumb specificity}: CTF3 and
CTF5 reviewers each named hints they wanted ``more structured''
or ``clearer direction'' for early-game recon, suggesting AI
tools partly substituted for breadcrumbing the suite did not
supply directly.
\textbf{(iii) Free-text identified actionable structural and
silent-data issues, all of which were resolved before
submission.} The CTF5 reviewer flagged that flags~3 and~4
shared a backing file (\texttt{/app/secret/flag.txt}) so an
RCE on \texttt{v1} rendered the WAF-bypass flag~3 trivially
skippable; this was fixed by separating the two flags onto
distinct files and gating flag~3 retrieval through the
\texttt{v2} WAF-protected path. The CTF9 reviewer reported
two silent data-seeding failures (an unresolved
\texttt{\{\{PLAYER\_FLAG2\}\}} template in
\texttt{application.properties}, and an empty
\texttt{flag\_value} for \texttt{flag\_index=4}) that returned
empty-format flags rather than failing closed; both were
fixed in the seeding pipeline so that every per-user flag is
populated at startup and a startup health-check now refuses
to bring the stack up if any expected flag is absent. The
CTF9 path-traversal ``god-mode shortcut'' (whereby
\texttt{/api/files/download?name=} accepted absolute paths
with no base-path restriction, allowing
\texttt{/app/seed/flags.json} to dump every flag) was
restricted to a whitelisted set of intended-solve paths,
preserving the legitimate JWT public-key and config reads
required by the chain. The CTF9 vault file
(\texttt{classified-trial-results-2024-q2.enc}) inaccessibility
was traced to a base-path mismatch and corrected. Each fix
is recorded against the relevant \textit{Post-Design Audit}
appendix.

The breadcrumbs cited as ``the artefact that moved me forward''
in free text were Rachel's security memo (CTF9), the in-page
HTML source comments and \texttt{/static/CHANGELOG.md} (CTF5),
the JWT \texttt{hr\_token} cookie inspection (CTF3), and the
session cookie (CTF1) --- which corroborates the methodology's
``breadcrumb-only'' progression principle
(Section~\ref{sec:design-philosophy}).

\subsubsection{Difficulty calibration}
\label{sec:eval-difficulty}

Difficulty bands (Easy/Medium/Hard) were assigned at design
time from a heuristic combination of stack count, flag count,
and intended-chain depth (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}); they
were not validated against an instrument. The four-participant
human walkthrough is the first piece of post-hoc calibration
evidence (Table~\ref{tab:human-outcomes}): the assigned
ordering is monotonic with respect to both time-to-flag (20\,m
Easy, 25\,m Medium, 40--80\,m Hard) and AI-tool reliance (Easy
solved unaided; Medium and both Hard solved with AI assistance,
and only partially on Hard). The Hard tail also separates
internally: CTF5 yielded a 3/4 partial in 40 minutes whereas
CTF9 yielded a 4/6 partial in 80 minutes by the most-experienced
participant, supporting CTF9's intended position as the hardest
artefact. A larger sample is needed to estimate variance and
confirm tier boundaries; the deferred multi-model LLM trial
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-llm}) is the planned second
calibration estimator.

\subsection{Implementation issues and resolutions}
\label{sec:eval-impl}

Three issues were material to the evaluation itself. First,
the e2e harness's initial naive \texttt{time.sleep(10)}
stack-ready pattern was flaky in CI and was replaced by a
polling \texttt{wait\_for\_service} on health endpoints
(\texttt{conftest.py:13}), with a 60\,s deadline and 2\,s
interval. Second, flag personalisation interacts non-trivially
with dynamic content: CTF3's AES-CBC leak test is sensitive to
Vite bundle chunking, and the resulting historical failure
against the \texttt{test\_encryption\_key\_in\_bundle} assertion
is recorded in \texttt{.pytest\_cache/v/cache/lastfailed};
[insert disposition]. Third, the multi-model agentic LLM panel
specified in \texttt{run\_matrix.py} was deferred on cost
grounds (roughly USD~9 per nine-CTF flagship run vs.\ a
\pounds20 envelope); the harness, prompts, run matrix and
committed expected-flag map make the deferred panel fully
replayable when budget allows. The per-challenge
\textit{Post-Design Audit} appendices
(Section~\ref{sec:dev-audit}) record audit cycles that found
and fixed unintended paths during development; CTF4
accumulated the most extensive trail (eleven findings, in a
separate \texttt{UNINTENDED\_vulnerabilities.md}).

\subsection{Strengths, limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:eval-limits}

\textbf{Strengths.} (i) Every challenge has an executable
intended-exploit assertion, which is strictly stronger than
natural-language solution guides. (ii) The HMAC flag layer
turns the integrity claim from a stated property into a
falsifiable measurement: a flag is solved only when the
submitted string is byte-equal to the per-deployment,
per-user HMAC value, so any methodologically correct but
hallucinated submission is recorded as a categorical failure.
(iii) The three tracks triangulate on the same nine-challenge
artefact: e2e exploit-as-a-test for objective ground truth, an
agentic harness for an integrity probe (deferred execution),
and a structured human walkthrough for process evidence.
(iv) The standardised \textit{Post-Design Audit} record per
challenge documents unintended-path discovery and resolution
in a comparable schema across the suite.

\textbf{LLM panel deferred.} The multi-model multi-seed agentic
panel specified in \texttt{run\_matrix.py} was deferred on hard
API budget grounds and remains replayable from committed
artefacts; until the trial is executed, the integrity claim is
supported by construction (HMAC over deployment salt and
username) rather than by direct empirical falsification under
LLM-assisted attack.
\textbf{Human-cohort sample.} $n=4$ is below the target range
($n=6$--$10$) and single-institution; reported effect sizes are
descriptive rather than inferential, and per-CTF coverage is
single-participant. Recruitment difficulty is the principal
contributor; a larger study with cohort-scale participation is
identified as further work in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
\textbf{No NASA-TLX in survey.} The fielded post-CTF survey
captured outcome, time, AI-tool use, confidence shift,
enjoyment and learning Likerts, and free text, but not the
NASA-TLX workload battery; per-task workload is therefore not
estimated and is identified as further work.
\textbf{Salt secrecy.} The integrity claim depends on the
deployment salt remaining secret; if a student obtains it
through misconfiguration or a shared-host filesystem read, the
HMAC layer collapses to a deterministic function of public
inputs and the property fails.
\textbf{Unintended-path coverage.} Automated tests assert
intended exploits only; the standardised \textit{Post-Design
Audit} record shipped in every challenge's
\texttt{SOLUTIONS.md} (Section~\ref{sec:dev-audit}) is the only
systematic search for alternative paths, and although the
schema makes records comparable across challenges, an
undiscovered bypass cannot be ruled out.
\textbf{Self-report bias.} Likert items are subjective and prone
to acquiescence, especially when the artefact's author runs the
session; where free-text and behavioural evidence diverge, the
behavioural reading is preferred.
\textbf{No control arm.} The ``learning gain over status quo''
claim is made by construction against the literature critique of
fixed-VM images, not by a randomised between-groups comparison
against the COMP2211 baseline; such a study is identified as
further work.
\textbf{Partial pre-registration.} The human track's protocol
is fixed but the analysis plan was not formally locked.

\subsection{Reflection on software engineering process and project organisation}
\label{sec:eval-se}

The suite was delivered under a three-phase plan recorded
in \texttt{CHANGELOG.md}: challenge-by-challenge build,
stack isolation and orchestration (\texttt{docker-compose.yml}
per CTF), and evaluation. Git hygiene was maintained on
\texttt{main} throughout with
tagged per-challenge commits, and the
commit log is a useful secondary record of which audit cycle
fixed which unintended path. The evaluation harness was built
before the LLM and human trials, which forced the executable
success criterion to be agreed in code before it could be
argued about in prose --- an intentional application of the
Dewey--Kolb experiential-learning framing~\cite{Dewey1938,kolb1984}
to the project's own process. The principal organisational risk was over-scoping: nine
challenges plus three evaluation tracks plus pre-registration
is a large surface, and the LLM agentic-panel execution was
deferred to keep the dissertation within budget. The harness
itself was completed and tested
(\texttt{Evaluation/llm/}, 102 unit tests) so that the
empirical integrity probe can be replayed without further
engineering; if a single decision were to be revisited, it
would be to ring-fence an additional budget envelope for a
multi-seed agentic run on a flagship model so that the
integrity row has a measured confidence interval rather than a
construction-by-design argument.

\subsection{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:eval-summary}

Read against the three project claims:
(a) \emph{Coverage} is supported --- the e2e suite asserts all
23 flags across all nine challenges (CTF8's third via a
contract assertion rather than the intended e2e chain;
Table~\ref{tab:e2e-coverage}), and the suite spans eight of the
ten 2021 OWASP categories across nine distinct technology
stacks;
(b) \emph{Integrity} is supported by construction --- every
flag is HMAC-derived from the per-deployment salt and the
recipient's username, so any byte-correct submission must have
exercised the live per-user chain. Empirical falsification
under LLM-assisted attack is deferred to the multi-model
agentic panel committed at \texttt{Evaluation/llm/};
(c) \emph{Pedagogical suitability} is partially supported by
the four-participant human walkthrough
(Table~\ref{tab:human-outcomes}): the assigned difficulty
ordering is monotonic on time-to-flag and AI-tool reliance,
enjoyment Likerts cluster 8--10/10 across the suite, and the
breadcrumbs cited as ``the artefact that moved me forward'' in
free text (Rachel's security memo, in-page source comments,
the \texttt{/static/CHANGELOG.md} keyword list, the JWT
\texttt{hr\_token} cookie) corroborate the methodology's
breadcrumb-only progression principle. A larger-scale human
study and the deferred LLM panel are identified as further
work in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
