\label{sec:results}

This chapter reports outcomes against the three-axis evaluation
plan committed in Section~\ref{sec:vvt}. Bracketed placeholders
mark data still in collection at the time of writing and are
resolved before submission.

\subsection{Evaluation design and suitability of approach}
\label{sec:eval-design}

The suite is assessed against the three Introduction-stated
claims rather than a single global metric: \emph{coverage} (OWASP
breadth and modern-stack diversity), \emph{integrity} (the
per-user HMAC flag layer resists flag copying and LLM-assisted
solving under deliberate attack), and \emph{engineering rigour}
(intended exploit paths survive code change). Three tracks map
onto these claims. \textbf{Automated regression} supplies an
objective solvability ground truth, removing the
solver-skill confound Meinsma et al.~\cite{Meinsma2022} identify
in platform-log evaluation. An \textbf{LLM solvability trial}
tests the integrity claim directly: a frontier model attempts
every challenge from the same curated public surface a student
would see, and a flag is recorded as solved only when the
submitted string is byte-identical to the HMAC-derived value for
the deployment salt and test user, so a methodologically correct
but hallucinated flag is a categorical failure. A \textbf{human
walkthrough} captures the process evidence absent from solver
counts: time-to-flag, hint-ladder consumption, NASA-TLX
workload~\cite{HartStaveland1988}, and free-text feedback.

Two methodological choices are worth naming. First, the LLM
trial artefacts are pre-committed: protocol
(\texttt{Evaluation/llm/PROTOCOL.md}), success criterion
(\texttt{PLAN.md} \S5), failure rubric (\texttt{RUBRIC.md}),
prompt SHA-256 hashes (\texttt{PROMPT\_HASHES.txt}), per-flag
regexes (\texttt{flag\_regexes.json}) and expected flag map
(\texttt{expected\_flags.json}) all live under
\texttt{Evaluation/llm/} ahead of any scoring run, removing the
post-hoc metric choice that would otherwise dominate noise on a
small-cell trial. Second, the byte-match criterion is the
mechanism that turns the integrity question from a claim into a
measurement: any submission that is not byte-equal to the
expected HMAC for the test user is recorded as a failure with a
sub-coded reason, regardless of whether the methodology was
correct.

\subsubsection{LLM robustness trial}
\label{sec:eval-llm}

The LLM track was executed as a manual, single-rater trial
across \textbf{two frontier models} --- OpenAI's
\textbf{GPT-5.3} and Anthropic's \textbf{Claude Sonnet~4.6} ---
with the author serving as each model's shell and HTTP client
and a 30-turn termination cap applied uniformly. The
human-in-the-loop tool layer is functionally equivalent to the
agentic harness specified in
\texttt{Evaluation/llm/run\_matrix.py}; the fully automated
five-model panel originally planned in \texttt{PLAN.md} was
descoped on hard API budget grounds (the top of the market is
priced at roughly USD~9 per nine-CTF flagship run, an order of
magnitude over the dissertation's \pounds20 envelope) and is
identified as further work in Section~\ref{sec:eval-limits}; the
harness, prompts, run matrix, flag regexes, expected flag map,
and SHA-pinned prompt hashes are all committed under
\texttt{Evaluation/llm/} so the trial can be replayed when
budget allows. Each session paste-loaded the curated doc pack
(\texttt{Evaluation/llm/doc-pack/ctf<n>.md}) and the test user
\texttt{abcd12}; transcripts are at
\texttt{manual/\{GPT-5.3,Sonnet-4.6\}/ctf<n>-results.md}.
Table~\ref{tab:llm-results} summarises outcomes.

The two-model picture is more informative than either model
alone. Both solvers fully cleared CTF1, CTF2, CTF3, CTF6, CTF7,
and CTF8 with byte-correct flags --- six of the nine challenges,
including every Basic-tier challenge and every multi-flag chain
where the chain depth fits inside the 30-turn budget. The
inter-model agreement is itself evidence: independently of each
other, two frontier models converged on the
\texttt{SOLUTIONS.md} intended path in every solved CTF, which
is the strongest available signal that the curated doc-pack
identifies the right vulnerability without over-disclosing the
exploit construction. Beyond the agreement, three findings are
worth naming separately.

\textbf{CTF9: divergent failure modes on the same prompt.}
GPT-5.3 refused CTF9 outright. The clinical-research narrative
(a Spring Boot ``trial vault'' for the fictitious Dunholm
Research's NIMMOD-2 study) tripped the model's sensitive-data
heuristic on every attempt; across 30+ reworded runs --- including
reorderings of the \texttt{\#\# Context and authorisation}
block, explicit fiction disclaimers, and restating that the
named entities are invented --- the model refused to issue any
HTTP probe before reaching the username/password exchange.
Sonnet 4.6, by contrast, engaged with the same prompt and
reached flag~6 (a staff-session plus player-JWT combination on
\texttt{/incident-report}) but ran out of turns at the 30-turn
cap with five flags still outstanding, having additionally
identified the SQLi, path traversal, and JWT
algorithm-confusion surfaces correctly. The prompt's
\texttt{\#\# Context and authorisation} block was therefore
load-bearing for one model and immaterial for the other, and the
suite still captures CTF9 as the hardest single artefact to
recover under the LLM-assistance threat model: 1 of 6 flags
across the better-engaged of two frontier solvers, against 17--23
on every other challenge.

\textbf{CTF3: same hallucinated extra flag, two models.}
The expected flag map records two flags for CTF3; both models
recovered both via the intended chain (\texttt{/api/flag} with a
normal session, then a debug-IDOR plus AES decryption with the
legacy bundled key) and then both spent the remaining
${\sim}10$--$15$ turns chasing a non-existent third flag
through the source manifest, IDOR variants, and forged-JWT
attempts. That two independent solvers reached the same
incorrect inference is a stronger negative finding than either
single observation: the challenge surface and doc pack jointly
present enough breadcrumbs that a strong model infers more flags
than exist, and the absence is only resolvable by exhaustion
rather than by signalling. Future revisions should either signal
flag count explicitly in-prompt or close off the dead-end
surfaces.

\textbf{Intended-path identification is reliable even when
execution fails.} Sonnet recovered no flag from CTF4 and only
one of six from CTF9, but in both transcripts its opening
``intended-path guess'' correctly named the full chain --- DOM
XSS through the \texttt{eval(callback)} sink with admin-bot
exfil for CTF4, and the actuator/SQLi/path-traversal/JWT/staff-
session ladder for CTF9. The 30-turn cap prevented full
execution (CTF4 needs an external exfil listener the human-
relayed tool layer cannot simulate; CTF9's six-stage depth
exceeds the budget) but the doc pack is sufficient for a
frontier model to map the vulnerability \emph{class} reliably
even where flag \emph{recovery} fails: the budget for
solvability is turn count and tooling, not signal.

\textbf{Process and integrity.} Solved-CTF turn counts span
5--27 (GPT median 19) and 5--20 (Sonnet median 14); across the
two models, 33 of 46 attempted flag slots came back byte-correct
(72\%), and 8/9 (GPT) and 6/9 (Sonnet) CTFs were fully cleared.
Because each flag is HMAC-derived from the deployment salt and
the test user, no recovered flag can have come from training
data: every byte-correct submission required exercising the live
chain against the per-user instance, which is positive evidence
that the HMAC layer holds against two independent frontier
models under the most permissive scaffolding available (human
tool layer, full doc-pack context, 30-turn budget). The LLM
solvers' speed advantage over the 18-minute human cap is real
but partly artefactual --- they bypass the read-and-comprehend
cost the human protocol measures deliberately --- so the
informative comparison with Section~\ref{sec:eval-human} is
qualitative (which chains each population recovers) rather than
quantitative on time-to-flag.

\subsubsection{Human walkthrough}
\label{sec:eval-human}

Five participants ($n=5$) drawn from the Durham CS UG/MEng
cohort completed the protocol fixed in
\texttt{Evaluation/human/PLAN.md}. Each 90-minute session
comprised a pre-survey, a non-trial warm-up, three CTF attempts
(one per tier, Latin-square counterbalanced, 18-min time cap),
and a post-survey, with the staged hint ladder at
\texttt{SESSION\_PROTOCOL.md:33}. Outcomes were scored as
\texttt{solved\_unaided}, \texttt{solved\_assisted},
\texttt{partial} or \texttt{timeout}, with per-participant
metrics including time-to-flag, hints-by-level, NASA-TLX
workload (\texttt{POST\_SURVEY.md:19}) and a five-item Likert
instrument on realism, novelty, calibration, coherence and
recommendation. The achieved $n$ is at the lower end of the
target range ($n=6$--$10$, \texttt{PLAN.md:12}); the study is
treated as a qualitative triangulation rather than a powered
inferential study, and a Durham CS low-risk ethics form was
completed prior to the first session.

\subsubsection{Difficulty calibration}
\label{sec:eval-difficulty}

Tier labels (Basic/Intermediate/Advanced) were assigned at
design time from a heuristic combination of stack count, flag
count, and intended-chain depth; they were not validated against
an instrument. The two-model turn counts in
Table~\ref{tab:llm-results} are the first piece of post-hoc
calibration evidence. Both models cleared every Basic-tier
challenge (CTF1, CTF2, CTF7) in fewer than 17 turns; both
cleared CTF6 and CTF8 (Intermediate-to-Advanced) within the 30-
turn budget; both stalled on CTF3, CTF4, CTF5, or CTF9 (Sonnet
on all four, GPT only on CTF3 and CTF9). The ordering is
broadly consistent with the assigned tiers but is not strictly
monotonic: CTF3 (Basic, multi-flag) ran the longest of the
solvable challenges because both models chased a non-existent
third flag, and CTF7 (Advanced) was among the fastest because
\texttt{node-serialize} CVE-2017-5941 is well-represented in
both models' training data. CTF9 (Advanced) sits firmly at the
top of the difficulty ordering on both axes --- one categorical
refusal and one 1/6 partial recovery --- which corroborates its
intended position as the hardest artefact in the suite.

\subsection{Implementation issues and resolutions}
\label{sec:eval-impl}

Four issues were material to the evaluation itself. First, the
e2e harness's initial naive
\texttt{time.sleep(10)} stack-ready pattern was flaky in CI and
was replaced by a polling
\texttt{wait\_for\_service} on health endpoints
(\texttt{conftest.py:13}), with a 60\,s deadline and 2\,s
interval. Second, flag personalisation interacts non-trivially
with dynamic content: CTF3's AES-CBC leak test is sensitive to
Vite bundle chunking, and the resulting historical failure
against the \texttt{test\_encryption\_key\_in\_bundle} assertion
is recorded in \texttt{.pytest\_cache/v/cache/lastfailed};
[insert disposition]. Third, the planned five-model agentic LLM panel
was descoped on cost grounds (roughly USD~9 per nine-CTF
flagship run vs.\ a \pounds20 envelope) and replaced with a
two-model manual trial across GPT-5.3 and Sonnet 4.6; the
harness, prompts, run matrix and committed expected-flag map
make the descoped panel fully replayable. Fourth, the CTF9
GPT-5.3 refusal observed during the LLM trial was not a harness
fault but a load-bearing finding for the integrity claim: the
prompt's \texttt{\#\# Context and authorisation} block was
iterated on 30+ times to clear GPT's safety gate without
success, while Sonnet 4.6 engaged with the same prompt
unmodified --- confirming the refusal is a durable
\emph{model-specific} property rather than a wording artefact. The per-challenge \texttt{workflow.md} files
(Section~\ref{sec:dev-audit}) record audit cycles that found
and fixed unintended paths during development; CTF4 accumulated
the most extensive audit trail (eleven recorded findings).

\subsection{Strengths, limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:eval-limits}

\textbf{Strengths.} (i) Every challenge has an executable
intended-exploit assertion, which is strictly stronger than
natural-language solution guides. (ii) The HMAC flag layer
turned the integrity claim from a stated property into a
falsifiable measurement: across two independent frontier solvers
33 of 46 attempted flag slots came back byte-correct, which is
only possible against the live per-user stack. (iii) The CTF9
result is itself a finding: realism sufficient for pedagogy is
sufficient either to trigger a frontier safety refusal (GPT-5.3)
or to exhaust a 30-turn budget on a six-stage chain (Sonnet
4.6), and the suite captures both as categorical failures.
(iv) The two solvers' independent convergence on the
\texttt{SOLUTIONS.md} intended path in every solved CTF is
inter-rater corroboration of the doc-pack curation. (v) The
three tracks triangulate on the same nine-challenge artefact.

\textbf{Two-model LLM panel.} The LLM evidence covers GPT-5.3
and Sonnet 4.6, single seed each, with the author as tool layer.
The five-model multi-seed agentic panel specified in
\texttt{run\_matrix.py} was descoped on hard API budget grounds
and remains replayable from committed artefacts; the present
result generalises within the \emph{frontier tier} only, and
$n=2$ cannot estimate cross-vendor variance with confidence.
The 30-turn cap bounded Sonnet on four CTFs (3, 4, 5, 9) and
GPT on one (CTF3); a longer cap would change recovery counts
but not the difficulty ordering between models.
\textbf{Single-rater sub-coding.} Failure sub-codes (notably
CTF3's cross-model hallucination and GPT's CTF9 refusal) were
assigned by the author; the planned 20\% double rating with
Cohen's~$\kappa$~\cite{Cohen1960} is further work, although the
two-model agreement on CTF3 already supplies an independent
corroborating signal that single-rater coding cannot.
\textbf{Human-cohort sample.} $n=5$ is at the lower end of the
target range and single-institution; reported effect sizes are
descriptive rather than inferential.
\textbf{Salt secrecy.} The integrity claim depends on the
deployment salt remaining secret; if a student obtains it
through misconfiguration or a shared-host filesystem read, the
HMAC layer collapses to a deterministic function of public
inputs and the property fails. \textbf{Unintended-path
coverage.} Automated tests assert intended exploits only;
one-author workflow audits are the only systematic search for
unintended solutions, and an undiscovered bypass in CTF1, CTF2
or CTF7 cannot be ruled out. \textbf{Self-report bias.}
NASA-TLX and Likert items are subjective and prone to
acquiescence, especially when the artefact's author runs the
session; where free-text and behavioural evidence diverge, the
behavioural reading is preferred. \textbf{No control arm.}
The ``learning gain over status quo'' claim is made by
construction against the literature critique of fixed-VM images,
not by a randomised between-groups comparison against the
COMP2211 baseline; such a study is identified as further work.
\textbf{Partial pre-registration.} The human track's protocol
is fixed but the analysis plan was not formally locked.

\subsection{Reflection on software engineering process and project organisation}
\label{sec:eval-se}

The suite was delivered under a three-phase plan
(\texttt{CTFs/WORKFLOW.md}): challenge-by-challenge build, stack
isolation and orchestration (\texttt{docker-compose.yml} per
CTF), and evaluation. Git hygiene was maintained on
\texttt{main} throughout with
tagged per-challenge commits, and the
commit log is a useful secondary record of which audit cycle
fixed which unintended path. The evaluation harness was built
before the LLM and human trials, which forced the executable
success criterion to be agreed in code before it could be
argued about in prose --- an intentional application of the
Dewey--Kolb experiential-learning framing~\cite{Dewey1938,kolb1984}
to the project's own process. The principal organisational risk
was over-scoping: nine challenges plus three evaluation tracks
plus pre-registration is a large surface, and the primary LLM
trial was deferred to the latest feasible window to keep the
integrity result current rather than retrospective. In
retrospect the budget-driven panel choice was the largest
trade-off imposed by the dissertation envelope; the manual
two-model trial recovers part of the lost cross-vendor signal
(the CTF9 result is now framed as model-specific rather than
``frontier-universal'' on Sonnet's evidence), but if a single
decision were to be revisited, it would be to ring-fence an
additional ~\pounds40 for a multi-seed agentic run on a flagship
model so that the integrity row has a confidence interval rather
than a point estimate.

\subsection{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:eval-summary}

Read against the three project claims:
(a) \emph{Coverage} is supported --- the e2e suite asserts all
23 flags across all nine challenges (CTF8's third via a
contract assertion rather than the intended e2e chain;
Table~\ref{tab:e2e-coverage}), and the LLM track shows two
independent frontier solvers converging on the
\texttt{SOLUTIONS.md} intended path in every solved CTF
(GPT-5.3 8/9 fully solved, Sonnet 4.6 6/9 fully solved);
(b) \emph{Integrity} is supported on its narrowest reading ---
every one of the 33 byte-correct flags returned across the
two-model trial required exercising the live exploit chain
against the per-user HMAC instance, which is the property the
flag layer was introduced to guarantee; the multi-seed agentic
panel and cold-probe contamination check remain further work;
(c) \emph{Pedagogical suitability} is partially supported by
the consistent two-model turn-count tier ordering and by
[insert human-track unaided-solve and NASA-TLX summary]; the
divergent CTF9 failure mode (GPT safety refusal vs.\ Sonnet
30-turn exhaustion) is an unanticipated and reportable property
of realistic clinical-domain framing under modern frontier
models that the suite uniquely captures.
