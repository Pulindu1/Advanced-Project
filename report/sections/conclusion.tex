\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This dissertation set out to answer how modern web application
vulnerabilities can be authentically modelled and pedagogically
structured within Capture-the-Flag environments to enhance
learning outcomes in cybersecurity education. The answer advanced
here is a nine-challenge suite spanning eight of the ten
2021 OWASP categories---including under-represented classes
such as server-side template injection, server-side request
forgery against simulated cloud metadata, and insecure
deserialisation---with a deterministic-HMAC personalisation
layer that makes flag sharing attributable and makes
LLM-generated flags structurally invalid, packaged as
self-contained Docker Compose stacks whose intended exploit
paths are verified by a three-layer test apparatus (per-CTF
unit and integration suites, an end-to-end exploit harness,
and a nine-job CI matrix).

\subsection{Main findings}

Three findings stand out from the evaluation.

First, \textit{ground-truth testing of exploitation is feasible and
cheap}. Writing a Python script that drives each challenge's
intended exploit chain end-to-end and asserts the presence of the
per-user flag in the response body produces an objective,
repeatable measure of solvability that a conventional unit-test
suite cannot. The scripts serve simultaneously as regression
tests, as evidence for markers, and as the controlled baseline
against which the LLM track is interpreted. This pattern
generalises well beyond the present suite.

Second, \textit{flag-layer personalisation is a surprisingly strong
integrity posture at a fraction of the cost of full environment
randomisation, and survives direct empirical falsification under
agentic LLM attack}. The Chothia--Novakovic SecGen
framework~\cite{chothia2023} correctly identifies per-student
randomisation as the strong answer to academic integrity, but the
full cost of SecGen-style VM parameterisation is disproportionate
for a web-specific graded module. Restricting personalisation to
the flag layer (with username embedded in every flag and a
per-deployment salt that invalidates write-ups across years) gives
up the methodology-sharing defence in exchange for a marked
reduction in operational overhead. The 5-model cold-probe
returned 0/121 byte-matches with no model fabricating even a
regex-shape guess across the 325-row failure denominator,
direct evidence that the HMAC-tail construction distinguishes
capability from regurgitation under the conditions tested. For
web-exploitation coursework, where methodology sharing
generally still requires competence to act on, this is a
pragmatic trade.

Third, \textit{scaffolding design is the main determinant of
pedagogical accessibility on the harder challenges}. The
human walkthrough's free-text responses repeatedly named
breadcrumbing artefacts (Rachel's security memo on CTF9, the
in-page HTML comments and \texttt{/static/CHANGELOG.md} on
CTF5, the JWT cookie inspection on CTF3) as ``the artefact
that moved me forward''---and equally, the lower learning
ratings on the AI-assisted middle band tracked back to
breadcrumbs participants felt were not specific enough. This
reframes the design question: not ``how hard is the
vulnerability'' but ``how visible is the reasoning path that
solves it''.

\subsection{Contributions}

The full contributions list, with executed-trial numbers and
artefact pointers, is given in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-contributions}; in summary, the project
delivers a nine-CTF, eight-OWASP, nine-stack web-exploitation
suite with deterministic-HMAC flag personalisation, a
container-native distribution model, a three-layer
exploit-as-a-test harness wired through a nine-job CI matrix,
and a triangulated evaluation (automated regression, executed
multi-model agentic LLM trial, structured human walkthrough),
released as a single replayable repository.

\subsection{Further work}

Six strands of further work are worth pursuing.

\textit{Larger-scale human study with cohort-level
participation.} The four-participant walkthrough is
diagnostic; recruitment was the principal constraint. A
properly powered study against a full COMP2211 cohort, using a
normalised-gain statistic~\cite{hake1998} with a NASA-TLX
workload battery~\cite{HartStaveland1988} (deferred from the
fielded survey), would supply the measurement
Meinsma et al.~\cite{Meinsma2022} and
Vykopal et al.~\cite{Vykopal} argue is missing. Modest
incentives or an optional not-for-credit framing would help
move recruitment from convenience samples toward
representative coverage. Because the suite ships its own
objective solvability baseline, such a study can separate
``challenge was solvable'' from ``student solved it'' more
cleanly than prior platforms permit.

\textit{Larger-budget multi-seed multi-model LLM rerun.} The
executed trial ran $n=1$ per cell at USD\,5.25 with a 15-turn
agentic cap that absorbed 75 of 90 agentic flag-slot failures.
Four extensions follow: multi-seed coverage on the existing
panel ($\sim 3\times$ the present spend) to convert the
single-seed table into a within-cell variance estimate; a
flagship-tier column (Opus, GPT-5, Gemini Ultra) at primary
scale on a \pounds40--\pounds80 envelope to bound capability
from above; a longer turn cap (30--50 turns) to remove the
truncation artefact, motivated by the manual GPT-5.3
calibration pass under \texttt{Evaluation/llm/manual/} solving
eight of nine CTFs unbounded in 5--30 turns; and a
retrieval-augmented or memory-equipped condition
(\texttt{CTFAgent}-style~\cite{ji2025}). An
adversarial-evolution variant, in which each suite iteration
resists the best-known harness, would give the integrity
argument a moving-target form matching the real-world threat.

\textit{Statistical evaluation of learning gain.} A controlled
pre-/post-test comparison against the COMP2211 status-quo
fixed-VM baseline would convert the present
``learning gain by construction'' argument into a
between-groups measurement, addressing the absence of a
control arm noted in Section~\ref{sec:eval-limits}.

\textit{Broader OWASP coverage and a 2025 refresh.} A04
Insecure Design and A06 Vulnerable and Outdated Components
remain outside the current coverage --- the former is
difficult to flag-ify, the latter ages out of scope quickly.
Refreshing against the 2025 OWASP Top~10, which adds Software
Supply Chain Failures, is an obvious follow-on.

\textit{Environment-level randomisation for a subset of
challenges.} Extending the personalisation layer toward
SecGen-style~\cite{chothia2023} environment randomisation for a
subset of challenges would close the methodology-sharing gap
without replacing the whole flag-layer scheme.

\textit{An automated learning-aware marking script.} Flag
possession is necessary but not sufficient evidence of
learning, since a student paired with a capable agentic LLM
can in principle recover a byte-correct flag without
internalising the methodology. A natural next deliverable is a
markscheme that supplements the flag check with short per-CTF
reasoning prompts --- e.g.\ ``what \texttt{STORY.md}
breadcrumb led you to the WAF source?'', ``which property of
the JWT verifier admits the algorithm-confusion attack?'' ---
mechanically scored by a keyword rubric or by an LLM judge
prompted with the per-CTF \texttt{SOLUTIONS.md} ground truth.
This would weight discovery and narrative comprehension
alongside exploitation; the per-CTF \texttt{STORY.md} files
and \textit{Post-Design Audit} records already in the
repository supply the ground truth.

\subsection{Closing remark}

The contribution of this project is ultimately a concrete
demonstration that the three commonly-separated goals of CTF
design---vulnerability realism, pedagogical accessibility, and
academic integrity---can be pursued jointly in a single web-specific
coursework context, without either compromising any of them or
paying the full cost of per-student environment randomisation. The
suite produced here is, to the author's knowledge, the first
web-exploitation CTF corpus to combine
deterministic-HMAC personalisation, container-native distribution,
and ground-truth exploit-script testing in an evaluated form, and
it is offered as a reusable baseline for future work in
cybersecurity education.
